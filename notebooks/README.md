# Example notebooks

These Jupyter notebooks illustrate the individual parts of pyoptics with
examples whose physics is easy to follow, and visualize them extensively.
Wherever an analytic solution exists, the numerics are compared with it. The
notebooks are stored with their outputs, so the figures can be seen without
running anything (e.g. directly on GitHub).

## Overview by topic

The notebooks are numbered by topic, in the groups below.

- **Wave optics, propagation and diffraction:** [01](01_propagation.ipynb) scalar propagation, [02](02_vectorial_diffraction.ipynb) vectorial diffraction and high NA focusing, [03](03_tilted_planes.ipynb) tilted planes, [04](04_chirp_z_transform.ipynb) chirp-z transform and zoomed spectra, [05](05_WPM.ipynb) wave propagation method
- **Beams, modes and paraxial optics:** [06](06_gaussian_beams.ipynb) Gaussian beams, [07](07_beam_quality.ipynb) beam quality (ISO 11146, M²), [08](08_abcd_matrices.ipynb) ABCD matrices and resonators, [09](09_mode_analysis.ipynb) mode analysis and fibre modes
- **Thin films, coatings and polarization:** [10](10_interfaces_thin_films.ipynb) interfaces and thin films, [11](11_polarization.ipynb) Jones and Mueller calculus, [12](12_layer_stacks.ipynb) interactive layer stacks, [13](13_stack_transfer_functions.ipynb) stack transfer functions and the Goos-Hänchen shift, [14](14_coated_laser_mirror.ipynb) coated laser mirror
- **Imaging, aberrations and detection:** [15](15_imaging_zernikes.ipynb) imaging and Zernikes, [16](16_bases.ipynb) polynomial bases and fits, [17](17_psf_mtf_strehl.ipynb) PSF, MTF and Strehl ratio, [18](18_image_processing.ipynb) camera noise and image processing
- **Ray tracing:** [19](19_ray_tracing.ipynb) sequential ray tracing and a lithography lens, [20](20_polarization_raytrace.ipynb) polarization ray tracing with coatings, [21](21_lens_focus_waves.ipynb) rays to waves: the focus of a lithography lens
- **Interferometry, holography and wavefront sensing:** [22](22_phase_unwrapping.ipynb) phase unwrapping, [23](23_interferometry.ipynb) phase-shifting and Fourier-transform interferometry, [24](24_radius_interferometer.ipynb) radius of curvature interferometer, [25](25_shack_hartmann_vs_phase_diversity.ipynb) Shack-Hartmann vs. phase diversity, [26](26_holography.ipynb) digital holography, [27](27_holographic_microscopy.ipynb) holographic microscopy
- **Phase retrieval and beam shaping:** [28](28_phase_retrieval.ipynb) phase retrieval and phase diversity, [29](29_TIE.ipynb) transport of intensity equation, [30](30_IFTA.ipynb) IFTA beam shaping

## Running

<!-- docs:running-start -->
You need an installation of pyoptics as described in the main README, plus
matplotlib, Jupyter and (for notebook 12) ipywidgets, all contained in the
`notebooks` extra:

```bash
pip install -e ".[notebooks]"
jupyter lab notebooks/
```

The widgets of notebook 12 only work with a running kernel; static viewers
such as GitHub show the static figures stored below each interactive cell.

Run times on an ordinary laptop range from a few seconds to about a minute per
notebook (longest: 01 and 05), with these exceptions:

- about 5 minutes: 32 (lithography toolbox, many focus-exposure matrices and
  through-pitch series), 41 (telecentricity, some 200 polarization ray
  traces over the slit)
- about 3 minutes: 21 (rays to waves, angular spectrum propagation to many
  planes), 28 (phase retrieval, many HIO runs)
- about 2 minutes: 20 (polarization ray trace), 33 (lens aberrations, ray
  traces over the slit and Zernike sensitivities)
- about 1.5 minutes: 09 (mode analysis, basis fits and large LG expansions),
  25 (Shack-Hartmann vs. phase diversity), 26 (holography, many refocusing
  runs on 1024² padded grids), 31 (vectorial imaging lithography), 37 (mask
  models, RCWA over a grid of pitches and duty cycles)

Notebooks 14 (coated laser mirror), 24 (radius interferometer), 27
(holographic microscopy), 34 (Hopkins/SOCS), 35 (resist), 36 (rigorous mask)
and 38 (Jones pupils) take 20 to 30 seconds, 39 (source optimization) and 40
(OPC) about 10 seconds.

The FFTs (pyoptics.fft) use all CPU cores; FFT-heavy notebooks such as 04
(chirp-z transform) and 26 (holography) run about 30 to 45 % faster than with
single threaded FFTs. Set `pyoptics.fft.workers = 1` to use a single core.

To re-execute all notebooks and store their outputs:

```bash
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace notebooks/*.ipynb
```
